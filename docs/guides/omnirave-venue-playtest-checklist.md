# OmniRave Venue Playtest Checklist

Use this checklist when reviewing the real venue-build pass in-engine.

The purpose is not “does it compile?” The purpose is “does this feel like the approved venue in real life?”

## Global Checks

- The world scale feels real, not compressed.
- Screens feel physically integrated into the architecture.
- The venue does not read as a placeholder or blockout from any obvious approach angle.
- Close-range views still look materially rich, not just the spawn view.
- Side routes and traversal landmarks feel intentionally built, not merely left open.
- HUD placement does not hide the core stage read.

## Main Stage Checklist

### Spawn / Back Plaza

- The first reveal feels monumental.
- The stage silhouette immediately reads as the approved hero direction.
- The central screen feels enormous at the correct scale.
- The side approaches and plaza infrastructure support the festival illusion.

### Midfield / Crowd View

- The stage still feels premium and high-definition from a central audience position.
- The stage has visible depth behind and around the screen.
- The field-to-stage relationship feels like a real festival, not a shallow game arena.

### Side Routes

- Approaching from either side still preserves the hero stage identity.
- The stage does not collapse into flat geometry from lateral angles.
- The Underground entrance side and warehouse side both feel placed intentionally in the larger grounds.

### VIP Read

- VIP is visibly present in the same world.
- VIP feels elevated and premium.
- The terrace/basin relationship is legible.

### Main Stage Fail Conditions

- looks like a giant screen with some boxes around it
- silhouette only works from one angle
- feels like a generic EDM stage

## Underground Checklist

### Entrance / Stair Descent

- The above-ground entrance marker feels believable.
- The descent feels like going into a real lower venue, not teleporting into another room.
- The halfway stair handoff concept still makes spatial sense.

### Arrival Platform

- The first platform view clearly sells the railcar booth as the focal landmark.
- The tunnel shell feels old, hostile, and heavy.

### Track Floor

- The dance floor truly reads as being on abandoned tracks.
- Rails, ballast, concrete, brick, and industrial details are actually present.
- The side-mounted screen feels built into the railcar infrastructure.

### Catwalk / Tunnel Edges

- The VIP catwalk is physically legible.
- Side tunnel darkness and fencing create believable depth.
- The room feels like an abandoned system, not a themed stage set.

### Underground Fail Conditions

- feels like a nightclub with subway wallpaper
- the railcar looks like a simple prop box
- the tunnel is too clean or too simple

## P.L.U.R.R. Partay Checklist

### Entrance View

- The loading-door entrance reads as a real warehouse entry.
- The far-wall altar immediately anchors the room.
- The warehouse feels broad and tall enough.

### Central Floor

- The central floor feels open and communal.
- The altar and side platforms feel physically grounded in the room.
- The room reads as a warehouse first, rave fantasy second.

### Side Platforms / VIP

- All three side VIP platforms feel real and explorable.
- Railings, stairs, and elevation changes make sense.

### Kandi Korner / Cuddle Puddle

- Both side areas are distinct enough to be remembered as places.
- They do not feel like generic corners with renamed props.

### Decor / Atmosphere

- Hanging decor is dense enough to feel authored.
- The room feels hand-built by ravers, not uniformly decorated in one pass.
- The color fantasy does not erase the warehouse realism underneath.

### P.L.U.R.R. Fail Conditions

- looks like a generic neon party room
- feels childish or toy-like
- side spaces are implied rather than actually built

## Review Deliverables

For each venue, the implementation review should provide:

- reference image or crop
- matched runtime screenshot
- short delta note

If those are missing, the venue review is incomplete.
