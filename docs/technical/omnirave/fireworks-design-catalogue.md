# OmniRave — new fireworks design catalogue

Design draft · 16 September 2026 · 25 individual aerial designs

This phase defines the fireworks themselves: ascent, launch tail, explosion structure, falling trails, color evolution, smoke, and sound. It does not define a show, launch schedule, music synchronization, formations, launch positions, or a finale. Catalogue numbers are identifiers, not a firing order.

The two user-supplied effect charts are visual references. Their captions are reference material, not instructions. Names such as “wave,” “snowflakes,” and “silver dragon” are treated as visual prompts where the image alone does not establish a precise behavior. The designs below specify our interpretation explicitly.

The proposed starting direction is believable fireworks with rich festival colors. This is an art-direction assumption pending discussion. These are designs for digital effects; they contain no physical pyrotechnic construction instructions.

## What the replacement should feel like

A shell has weight on its way up, a brief, energetic break, and individual burning stars that keep moving as they lose energy. The viewer should be able to recognize the effect from its silhouette and movement before noticing its color.

- **Ascent:** a small incandescent head with a specifically designed tracer, visible acceleration/deceleration, and a sparse smoke wake. Most shells use a lift thump and tracer hiss. A rising whistle is a deliberate exception, not the default sound of every shell.
- **Explosion:** a quick light impulse followed by an authored distribution of stars. No visible solid sphere, polygonal orb, or uniform collection of oversized luminous rods.
- **Trails:** luminous material is emitted along each star's actual path, remains behind in world space, cools, and drifts. A trail must bend naturally rather than remain attached as a rigid line pointing at the burst center.
- **Light and color:** very small hot cores can approach white. The surrounding color remains legible. Warm metal trails cool through gold, amber, and dim ember; colored stars hold their intended hue and extinguish without a compulsory rainbow cycle.
- **Smoke:** a small break cloud and thin path-following wisps, briefly lit by the stars. Wind moves smoke and old trail particles consistently. Smoke must not become a permanent glowing ball.
- **Falloff:** the stars dim at slightly different moments; the last embers disappear naturally. Long-lived shells preserve their droop and falling shape through the end.
- **Sound:** every design has a named launch, break, and aftermath profile. Differences come from attack, weight, burn texture, secondary events, and decay—not just pitching the same boom up or down.

There are two different tails to design: the rising tracer behind the shell and the trails left by stars after the break. All 25 designs specify both. A peony still has its own rising tracer even though its burst stars deliberately have almost no trails.

## Individual designs

The pacing labels below describe the life of one firework after its own break. They are not show timings. Absolute scale, altitude, particle budgets, and durations will be calibrated with the actual venue camera during development.

### F01 — Ruby Peony

- **Identity:** a clean, open red sphere; medium spread and a short finish.
- **Ascent and tail:** restrained ruby head; narrow warm-silver tracer that becomes thinner near the apex. Most of the tracer fades before the sphere opens.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** evenly distributed red stars on a three-dimensional spherical shell, with slight irregularity in brightness and spacing. Almost trail-free heads travel outward, slow, and dim separately. The center opens into dark sky; it does not stay filled by a glowing ball.
- **Color and smoke:** deep ruby holds through most of the burn. A small warm break cloud is briefly visible, then becomes subdued grey.
- **Sound — `ruby_peony`:** dry low lift **thump**, restrained airy ascent hiss, a clean **crack** over a compact low boom, then a short outdoor decay. No added crackle bed.
- **Recognition test:** recognizable as a peony in monochrome because points, dark center, and empty spaces dominate.

### F02 — Sapphire Chrysanthemum

- **Identity:** a full spherical flower with many fine radial trails; medium spread and a lingering finish.
- **Ascent and tail:** a bright silver needle with a fine blue-edged tracer; a few silver grains detach along the ascent.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** blue heads pull silver trails through the opening sphere. Trails begin straight, progressively arc down, and fade behind still-visible tips. Front and rear stars establish depth.
- **Color and smoke:** sapphire tips remain distinguishable from silver trails; older trails warm slightly before disappearing. A thin smoky sphere outlasts the bright opening.
- **Sound — `sapphire_chrysanthemum`:** crisp lift report, fine rising **shhh**, a bright opening snap over a rounded boom, and a smooth silver-sizzle tail.
- **Recognition test:** the trail flower remains distinct from F01's point sphere without relying on color.

### F03 — Amethyst Dahlia

- **Identity:** fewer, larger violet stars; wide spacing and a deliberately uncluttered silhouette.
- **Ascent and tail:** small violet head on a short, grainy bronze tracer with visible gaps.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** large violet heads form a sparse sphere. Brief tapered wakes emphasize initial speed; most of the travel is read from the heads themselves. Stars retain presence as their outward motion slows.
- **Color and smoke:** violet bodies with tiny pale cores, never large white discs. Minimal detached sparks and a restrained break cloud.
- **Sound — `amethyst_dahlia`:** muffled lift **thoom**, coarse brief ascent breath, a woody full-bodied break, and a smooth low decay with little high-frequency fizz.
- **Recognition test:** dark gaps and larger individual stars distinguish it from both Peony and Chrysanthemum.

### F04 — Emerald Heart Pistil

- **Identity:** two concentric spherical layers with a dark band between them.
- **Ascent and tail:** green head with a tightly braided gold tracer; the gold disappears unevenly behind the head.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** a gold outer chrysanthemum opens around a smaller emerald sphere. Both originate from the same break; different outward speeds preserve the nested structure. The inner points expire before the final outer gold trails.
- **Color and smoke:** saturated emerald heart, warm gold perimeter, sparse smoke between layers so the center remains visible.
- **Sound — `emerald_heart`:** firm lift, restrained gold hiss, one layered **crack–boom**, followed by a short metallic shimmer. The two layers do not imply a second explosion.
- **Recognition test:** two distinct radii remain visible from the main viewing area and side views.

### F05 — Antique Gold Brocade

- **Identity:** a rich, dense crown built from fine sparkling trails; broad and substantial.
- **Ascent and tail:** warm white head and a dense gold tracer shedding small side flecks.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** many gold branches spread into a rounded crown. Each main trail sheds fine glitter that makes the crown look woven. The branches sag, overlap, and diminish into a broad ember canopy.
- **Color and smoke:** pale gold at the opening, antique gold through the body, copper-brown embers at the finish. Smoke is visible in the bright center but does not flatten the layered crown.
- **Sound — `antique_brocade`:** heavy rounded lift **thump**, coarse metallic ascent, a deep **whump** with a brief crack, then a dense sand-like sparkle hiss that thins gradually.
- **Recognition test:** density and small-scale branching distinguish it from Willow's separated strands.

### F06 — Champagne Willow

- **Identity:** a very broad canopy of exceptionally long, separated falling strands.
- **Ascent and tail:** slim champagne comet head with a quiet, steady tracer; trailing grains hang briefly behind it.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** a restrained opening spreads long-burning gold stars into a dome. Outward motion slows early while the strands continue falling, creating a weeping canopy. The longest paths remain thin and irregular.
- **Color and smoke:** pale champagne through gold to very dim orange at the tips. Wispy hanging smoke follows the same downward paths and ambient wind.
- **Sound — `champagne_willow`:** low lift **puff-thump**, soft ascent rush, a broad bass-heavy boom with a rounded attack, then a long, very quiet brushing hiss. The visible tail can outlast the audible burn.
- **Recognition test:** it remains recognizably drooping even after the initial burst is gone; density never turns it into Brocade.

### F07 — Copper Palm

- **Identity:** one pronounced rising trunk and a small set of thick, upward-opening fronds.
- **Ascent and tail:** strong copper-gold comet that leaves the thickest trunk-like rising tail in the catalogue.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** a sparse upper fan of heavy stars bends outward into fronds. Each frond has a hot leading tip and a granular wake. The trunk lingers beneath the crown, completing the palm silhouette.
- **Color and smoke:** warm white cores, copper bodies, falling amber grains. The trunk has its own thin smoke column; frond smoke stays sparse.
- **Sound — `copper_palm`:** punchy low lift, rough sustained **fshhh**, a chesty opening **thwump**, and several coarse, overlapping burn textures corresponding to the heavy fronds.
- **Recognition test:** trunk and open upper fronds are both readable; it must not become a full spherical shell.

### F08 — Golden Coconut

- **Identity:** sparse, thick golden spokes with prominent pearl-like tips; a rounder silhouette than Palm.
- **Ascent and tail:** a compact white-gold head over a narrower, broken gold tracer. The rising line fades before the spokes dominate.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** heavy comet stars open in many directions around a sphere. Bright rounded heads stay visible at the ends of long gold tails. The spokes bow downward while maintaining their separation.
- **Color and smoke:** warm white pearls over golden trails; isolated smoke fingers rather than a dense crown.
- **Sound — `golden_coconut`:** tight lift **tok-thump**, grainy short ascent, a hard central report with a hollow low body, then separated raspy comet burns over a medium decay.
- **Recognition test:** pearl tips and full radial spread distinguish it from the trunk-and-fronds Palm.

### F09 — Silver Spider

- **Identity:** a fast, wide opening with a small number of long silver legs.
- **Ascent and tail:** thin silver streak with a compact head and little lingering debris.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** stars race outward, leaving narrow bright paths, then visibly lose speed. Long legs droop at the tips while their inner sections fade. Controlled asymmetry makes the form feel energetic.
- **Color and smoke:** white-silver legs with faint warm terminal embers; smoke is initially swept outward, then drifts slowly.
- **Sound — `silver_spider`:** sharp lift snap, quick airy **zip**, a fast, aggressive opening crack, and a short bright tearing hiss over a compact bass body.
- **Recognition test:** the fast-to-slow movement and sparse long legs distinguish it from Chrysanthemum.

### F10 — Amber Waterfall

- **Identity:** a narrow aerial crown feeding a long downward curtain.
- **Ascent and tail:** understated amber tracer with a small bright head, leaving room for the curtain's shape.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** a compact opening separates into near-parallel hanging trails. Vertical descent dominates lateral expansion; different lengths form an uneven lower edge. Trails terminate in the air as their stars burn out.
- **Color and smoke:** warm white at the upper release, amber along the curtain, low orange at the trailing ends. A thin vertically extended smoke veil remains.
- **Sound — `amber_waterfall`:** low soft lift, faint ascent hiss, a rounded restrained opening boom, then a continuous granular **shhhhh** that gradually thins from the top of the falling mass.
- **Recognition test:** reads as a curtain, not a wide Willow dome. This is an aerial interpretation of the reference.

### F11 — White Strobe

- **Identity:** a loose sphere whose white stars independently blink into and out of view.
- **Ascent and tail:** short silver tracer with irregular bright flecks; it does not blink as a single solid line.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** a modest break establishes the star sphere. Stars keep moving through their dark intervals and reappear farther along the same paths. Different phases prevent a synchronized full-sphere flash. Trails remain extremely short.
- **Color and smoke:** white points, slight cool edges, little residual glow during dark intervals. Smoke is visible only around nearby bright stars.
- **Sound — `white_strobe`:** dry lift, fine hiss, a tight metallic break and airy decay. Flickering light does not automatically create a new pop; this profile has no continuous crackling layer.
- **Recognition test:** coherent trajectories remain evident across the dark intervals; brightness modulation owns the identity.

### F12 — Rose Gold Glitter

- **Identity:** an airy rounded crown covered in fine, irregular shimmer.
- **Ascent and tail:** rose-colored head with a gold dust tracer and tiny delayed glints.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** warm rose tips spread outward while their gold wakes shed many small glitter particles. The canopy shimmers continuously as it slowly drops; it never snaps into whole-star on/off strobing.
- **Color and smoke:** restrained rose accents over pale gold; smoke catches local glints without glowing uniformly.
- **Sound — `rose_glitter`:** soft granular lift, dry brushing ascent, a bright but rounded bloom of sound, and a delicate, even **sisss** with fine grit rather than isolated loud pops.
- **Recognition test:** shimmering trails separate it from White Strobe's blinking heads and Crackle's miniature explosions.

### F13 — Dragon-Egg Crackle

- **Identity:** a gold burst whose branch ends each erupt into tiny local flashes.
- **Ascent and tail:** chunky gold tracer with occasional sharp glitter grains.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** a medium gold sphere opens, then parent stars produce small secondary bursts distributed along the outer shape. Each secondary event has a tiny flash, a few very short sparks, and rapid extinction. The parent crown remains readable.
- **Color and smoke:** warm gold parents and pale-gold crackle flashes; small smoky puffs arise at the secondary events.
- **Sound — `dragon_egg_crackle`:** coarse lift thump, grainy ascent, a strong main report, then clearly separated **krr-pop-pop** clusters. Audible secondary pops follow the actual secondary events.
- **Recognition test:** locally expanding mini-bursts distinguish it from simple blinking particles.

### F14 — Golden Time Rain

- **Identity:** a hanging gold field that begins restrained and gradually releases fine crackling rain.
- **Ascent and tail:** dense but dim bronze tracer punctuated by small golden beads.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** slow gold parents spread into a broad canopy. Fine bright flecks appear progressively along the descending parents, producing a delayed granular rain with uneven depth. Each parent exhausts separately.
- **Color and smoke:** darker old gold at first, brighter brief gold flecks during the rain, then sparse bronze remnants. Smoke is extended downward and shared by the drifting flecks.
- **Sound — `golden_time_rain`:** low lift knock, husky ascent, a restrained deep opening boom, a perceptible lull in high-frequency detail, then a widening fine **prrrr** crackle bed that tapers gradually.
- **Recognition test:** gradual release and sustained falling texture distinguish it from Crackle's discrete terminal bursts.

### F15 — Jade Falling Leaves

- **Identity:** a gentle cloud of luminous green embers fluttering down at different rates.
- **Ascent and tail:** small jade head on a sparse silver tracer with little lingering brightness.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** a soft release makes a loose star cloud rather than a sharply defined sphere. Stars flutter laterally, slow quickly, and descend with tiny wakes. Brightness drifts smoothly. Individual points must not look like literal leaf meshes.
- **Color and smoke:** jade and softer yellow-green accents, no white burn-out wash. A loose smoke puff drifts with the whole cloud.
- **Sound — `jade_leaves`:** rounded small lift, whisper-like ascent, a muted **pup-boom**, and a restrained breathy tail that becomes almost silent while the embers continue falling.
- **Recognition test:** slow drift dominates; no fast darting Fish or Bees behavior.

### F16 — Silver Snowflakes

- **Identity:** small, airy clusters of white sparks settling like luminous snow.
- **Ascent and tail:** fine silver powder tracer, slightly wider and softer than Spider's needle.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** a restrained release creates widely separated groups of tiny sparks. Each group has a bright center and a few ephemeral micro-sparks, then descends gently. Group members twinkle softly without repeated hard flashes.
- **Color and smoke:** cool white centers and neutral silver dust. Low-density smoke stays behind the settling groups.
- **Sound — `silver_snowflakes`:** light papery lift, powdery ascent, a delicate crisp break over a shallow low body, followed by a fine dry brushing texture. No bells or synthetic chimes.
- **Recognition test:** grouped micro-sparks distinguish this authored reference interpretation from Strobe and Falling Leaves.

### F17 — Jade Crossette

- **Identity:** green parent comets that each split into four shorter children.
- **Ascent and tail:** narrow green-white tracer that sheds a few gold grains.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** a small set of parents fans out with visible green tails. At a defined point in each parent's life, four children split from its current location and retain its outward momentum. Their brief crossing trails read as local plus-shaped branches before gravity bends them.
- **Color and smoke:** jade parents; slightly paler green children with tiny warm break flashes. Each split leaves a small transient smoke puff.
- **Sound — `jade_crossette`:** firm lift, clean ascent hiss, a compact central report followed by short, dry secondary **tak** sounds from the split locations. Child sounds remain lower in weight than the main break.
- **Recognition test:** every parent splits into four from its actual moving position; no unrelated cross appears at the original center.

### F18 — Aqua Fish

- **Identity:** a modest central burst releasing stars that briefly swim away along curved paths.
- **Ascent and tail:** aqua head, narrow white tracer, and short detached blue-white flecks.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** stars open into a loose cloud, then each follows a smooth changing direction for a brief active phase. Short tails reveal local curves. After that phase, heads coast and fall; they do not continue steering indefinitely.
- **Color and smoke:** aqua heads with pale silver wakes; localized wisps trace their curves.
- **Sound — `aqua_fish`:** light lift thump, thin ascending breath, a modest bright break, then scattered short fluttering **fssht** textures with a soft low decay.
- **Recognition test:** short smooth independent turns distinguish Fish from the long continuous Silver Dragon branches.

### F19 — Amber Bees

- **Identity:** a tighter burst of fast gold heads that dart and scatter.
- **Ascent and tail:** bright amber head with a broken, grainy tail and a slightly rougher burn.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** many small heads leave a compact center, briefly accelerate along divergent paths, then dart through short curves. Their life is brisk; wakes are shorter and direction changes more abrupt than Fish. Motion stays finite and rooted in the break.
- **Color and smoke:** warm yellow-gold heads, copper grains, scattered small smoke trails.
- **Sound — `amber_bees`:** tight lift **pop**, rough ascent rush, a papery central crack, then brief raspy, uneven buzzing burn textures. Keep it combustible and airy, not an electronic insect loop.
- **Recognition test:** compact scale, brisk agitation, and short life distinguish it from Aqua Fish.

### F20 — Silver Dragon

- **Identity:** several silver serpents drawing long, continuously twisting paths.
- **Ascent and tail:** white-silver tracer with a subtle corkscrew in its shedding pattern; a restrained wavering whistle is this design's characteristic ascent sound.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** a small opening releases a few long-burning heads with finite rotating lateral thrust. Their continuous trails form elongated bends and loops as the whole assembly travels outward and down. The branches do not all rotate together.
- **Color and smoke:** white-hot tips, silver bodies, occasional amber flecks. Thin twisting smoke paths continue beyond the bright trails.
- **Sound — `silver_dragon`:** firm lift, airy wavering ascent whistle, a hard compact break, and several narrow, raspy descending burn textures that stop as the heads exhaust.
- **Recognition test:** long continuous individual serpents; no shared pinwheel center.

### F21 — Blue Jellyfish

- **Identity:** a rounded blue cap over a small number of long gold tendrils.
- **Ascent and tail:** small blue-white head over a restrained gold stem.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** an upper hemisphere of short-lived blue points opens while slower gold stars descend below it. The cap thins as the tendrils lengthen. The shape emerges from separate star populations with different velocities and lifetimes; it is never a translucent jellyfish mesh.
- **Color and smoke:** cobalt-to-aqua cap, champagne tendrils, faint cool-lit smoke near the top.
- **Sound — `blue_jellyfish`:** soft low lift, fine gold hiss, a deep rounded opening with a bright short cap of noise, then a gentle falling sizzle associated with the tendrils.
- **Recognition test:** the cap and lower tendrils overlap long enough to read together from the crowd view.

### F22 — Ruby Halo Ring

- **Identity:** a clean ring of separate red points around an empty center.
- **Ascent and tail:** dim ruby tracer with a silver needle at its head; the ascent line disappears quickly after the break.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** stars expand along a defined world-space plane. Their short wakes fade quickly, leaving a ring with slightly imperfect spacing. Gravity later lowers and softens the shape; the ring is not a rigid object.
- **Color and smoke:** ruby points, restrained silver pincores, a thin and quickly dispersing break cloud.
- **Sound — `ruby_halo`:** clean lift click-thump, short ascent hush, a precise dry report with a concise bass body and very little residual sparkle.
- **Recognition test:** it stays hollow. It is allowed to appear elliptical or nearly edge-on from side viewpoints; it must not secretly rotate to face each viewer.

### F23 — Violet Spiral

- **Identity:** a two-turn open spiral of separate violet stars with a pale silver accent near its center.
- **Ascent and tail:** violet head and a lightly twisted silver tracer, finer and quieter than Silver Dragon's.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** stars open along an authored spiral distribution in a shallow volume. Radius and angular placement establish the shape; short trailing glints reinforce it. The stars expand and fall independently, gradually loosening the spiral without rotating it as a solid logo.
- **Color and smoke:** violet outer points, limited silver inner accents, low smoke density through the gaps.
- **Sound — `violet_spiral`:** small resonant lift, thin rising hiss, a crisp opening report over a smooth rounded body, then a brief soft wavering sizzle. No added magical sweep.
- **Recognition test:** the gap between successive turns survives the opening and early decay. This is a stylized signature pattern with a defined viewing orientation.

### F24 — Silver Whirlwind

- **Identity:** a burst of curved silver spokes suggesting a rotating pinwheel.
- **Ascent and tail:** a thin bright head over a silver tail that sheds alternating small side flecks.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** stars combine outward travel with tangential motion for a finite interval. Their trails describe similarly handed arcs; individual heads then coast and drop. The visual center becomes empty as stars leave it. This design interprets the references labeled Spinner and Whirlwind.
- **Color and smoke:** silver arms with faint warm ends; curved wisps reinforce the swirl briefly.
- **Sound — `silver_whirlwind`:** crisp lift, fluttering ascent rush, a bright central crack and a coarse swirling burn envelope that decays into a low outdoor tail. Spatial motion follows sources, not an arbitrary stereo rotation.
- **Recognition test:** shared handedness distinguishes it from independently twisting Dragon branches; it must not look like a rotating mesh.

### F25 — Magenta Wave

- **Identity:** a broad open fan of magenta spokes with repeated shallow curves.
- **Ascent and tail:** magenta head with a smooth thin gold tracer and a restrained silver highlight.
- **Explosion and falling trails:** a fan of stars spreads in a shallow curved volume. A brief, small lateral oscillation shapes the trails into repeated sweeps; outward movement remains dominant. The star paths relax into falling arcs as they exhaust. This is an authored interpretation of the Wave reference.
- **Color and smoke:** magenta leading stars, subdued warm-silver trails, sparse curved smoke wisps.
- **Sound — `magenta_wave`:** low clean lift, soft rising rush, a broad papery report with a warm low body, then an airy undulating burn texture. The undulation is subtle and ends with the stars.
- **Recognition test:** open fan and repeated shallow sweeps distinguish it from Whirlwind's stronger angular circulation.

## Sound production plan

These are sound briefs, not completed audio assets. Each sound ID needs its own reviewed combination of launch, ascent, break, and aftermath. A profile can deliberately have a nearly silent aftermath.

1. Assemble or produce clean, licensed source recordings for lift reports, small and large breaks, tracer burns, comet burns, individual crackles, dense rain crackles, and outdoor decay. Record provenance for each external asset. Do not lift sounds from the supplied reference images' surrounding media or unrelated videos.
2. Build a separate layered profile for each catalogue ID. Shared source material is acceptable, but the resulting attack, tonal body, texture envelope, and secondary-event arrangement must match its brief. Small variations prevent obvious repetition without changing the identity.
3. Keep the main break mono or spatially coherent at the break location. Move ascent burn sources with the head. Keep large secondary clusters anchored to their actual split positions; group tiny crackles spatially to limit simultaneous voices.
4. Apply distance-dependent loudness, high-frequency loss, and sound travel delay from the event's world position. The visible break occurs first; the boom arrives later at distant listeners. Arrival timing is an acoustic property, not a show cue.
5. Use finite burn loops with smooth onset and release. Keep a restrained shared outdoor reflection treatment. Do not hard-bake a huge indoor reverb into every shell.
6. Provide a dedicated effects-volume control, bounded peak output, and a limited simultaneous voice budget. Respect the existing browser audio-unlock behavior. Never replay missed launch or explosion sounds as a backlog when audio unlocks late.
7. Review each design sound-only, picture-only, and together at the same representative listener position. Strobe brightness changes must not automatically trigger crackles; real splits and crackle bursts should carry matching sound events.

## Rendering and runtime plan

### Observed starting point

The current Babylon implementation uses pooled radial particle systems with stretched billboards, a separate hot-core particle system, six-segment rocket-head spheres, and eight-segment burst-flash spheres. Its main module also owns show phases, launch cadence, stage pyro, and sky lettering. The audio module exposes one generic launch and one generic explosion parameterized by intensity. The runtime dynamically imports the show and manages audio unlock.

Relevant existing files:

- `omnirave-babylon/src/scene/createFireworksShow.ts`
- `omnirave-babylon/src/audio/createFireworksAudio.ts`
- `omnirave-babylon/src/scene/createMainStagePresentationRig.ts`
- `omnirave-babylon/src/app/createRuntime.ts`
- `omnirave-babylon/src/scene/__tests__/createFireworksShow.test.ts`

These observations come from source inspection, not a new visual benchmark. The working tree already contains unrelated work and changes to the existing fireworks module. This planning pass leaves that implementation intact.

### Proposed new structure

- **Catalogue:** one immutable definition per firework: ID, geometry family, launch-tail recipe, star populations, color/burn curves, split rules, smoke recipe, sound profile, bounds, and quality variants. No event minute, launch interval, music cue, or finale field belongs in a definition.
- **Individual effect instance:** receives a definition ID, world transform, seed, and its own starting state. It owns a single shell's ascent, break, secondary events, and burnout. This local lifecycle is independent of a future show controller.
- **Simulation:** coherent three-dimensional initial distributions, time-step-stable motion, gravity and drag, shared wind, bounded active motion for Fish/Bees/Dragon, and deterministic split positions. Radius and density are independent controls.
- **Renderer:** batched luminous heads; continuous, tapered trails sampled from actual paths; a separate lightweight sparkle layer; bounded smoke; a brief small break flash. Trails store where material was emitted rather than stretching a single sprite into every arm.
- **Audio:** a reusable event renderer with per-design profiles. Primary and secondary sound events are generated from the same lifecycle events as the visuals.
- **Review scene:** manually trigger one selected firework, replay a seed, freeze or inspect a local phase, view without bloom, and switch between a neutral sky and the actual venue. This is a test bench, not a show sequencer.

Prefer pooled, batched rendering and shared materials. Do not create one mesh, particle system, or audio context per star. Use GPU work for dense secondary detail where practical, but choose the approach only after a representative primary-trail prototype is measured. The Babylon GPU-particle documentation lists limitations including sub-emitters and `manualEmitCount`; simply changing the current class to `GPUParticleSystem` is not an adequate replacement strategy. Validate the installed engine's WebGPU and WebGL behavior before committing to an implementation. [Babylon GPU particle documentation](https://github.com/BabylonJS/Documentation/blob/master/content/features/featuresDeepDive/particles/particle_system/gpu_particles.md).

All heads and trails must work within the venue's existing bloom and color pipeline. Measure the appearance with bloom disabled first, then tune it with the venue's normal settings. Fine sparkle detail may reduce at lower quality; silhouette, major branches, characteristic motion, and sound identity must survive.

The existing ascent endpoints and spread values are not new design targets. Calibrate apparent size, head size, trail width, height, and sound distance against the venue's actual scale and camera. Set particle, trail-segment, draw-call, CPU, GPU, and audio-voice budgets from that measured baseline rather than promising an untested particle count.

### Design-to-build process

1. Discuss the catalogue's artistic direction and individual identities. Resolve any changes to the roster and the more stylized interpretations before treating them as final assets.
2. Develop isolated proof pieces for Peony, Chrysanthemum, Willow, Crossette, and Crackle. Together these exercise points, continuous trails, long decay, branching, and sound-linked secondary bursts. This is a development dependency choice, not launch order.
3. Establish the shared visual and audio quality bar using those proofs. Then author the remaining definitions individually and make a separate visual/audio review record for every ID.
4. Validate in the actual venue at representative viewpoints, with both supported render paths and chosen quality levels. Run a bounded overlap stress test strictly for performance and cleanup; do not use it to choose a show sequence.
5. Once the replacement is ready for integration, remove the old aerial effect code, its generic audio path, its obsolete assets, and tests that assert placeholder implementation details. Identify any stage-pyro or lettering callers before removal so they are explicitly migrated or retained in their own owner. The goal is one active fireworks implementation after cutover.
6. Design show choreography, launch positions, cue timing, synchronization, and transitions in a later phase. They remain deliberately undecided in this document.

## Acceptance criteria for each design

- Its shape and movement can be identified in monochrome and with bloom disabled.
- The ascent tail and the post-break trails match the individual brief.
- Continuous trails have no visible polygonal kinks, bead chains, disconnected white cores, camera-facing shape tricks, or sudden resets when the camera moves.
- Secondary effects originate at the parents' actual positions and inherit consistent motion.
- Distinct color layers remain visible at venue exposure; hot cores do not bleach the entire firework.
- Long trails fade naturally with no synchronized cutoff, pool-reuse jump, or reset when an unrelated shell starts.
- Smoke and drift remain spatially consistent and clear completely after the effect dies.
- Sound has a distinct authored identity; break, split, and crackle events remain aligned after acoustic travel delay.
- Ring, Spiral, and other directional patterns are reviewed from front, side, and oblique viewpoints, with their viewing limitations recorded.
- Seed replay and different frame rates preserve the same effect identity and event ordering.
- Idle effects consume no active simulation or sound work; completion and scene teardown release owned resources.
- Lower quality removes secondary density before sacrificing the characteristic shape.

## Decisions kept open

- Final realism level and whether the stylized Spiral, Whirlwind, and Wave stay in the main palette.
- Any additions, removals, alternate colors, or renamed signature designs.
- Exact visual lifetimes, venue-relative size classes, viewing orientations, and audio mixes after isolated proofs exist.
