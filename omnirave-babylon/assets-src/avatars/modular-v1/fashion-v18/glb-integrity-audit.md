# Original GLB integrity audit

Date: 2026-08-31

Audited file: `export/avatar-base-editorial.glb`

SHA-256: `83168083d1845c3f9f11e917ac55dd0c12fac7624d0bbd866785b561fb777685`

## Verdict

The GLB is structurally coherent and loads correctly, but its animation payload is a limited placeholder rather than a production-quality locomotion set. Mesh segmentation, skin references, morph buffers, material references, and texture references are internally consistent. The most consequential source-asset problem is sparse animation authoring and resulting self-intersection, not a corrupted or fully merged GLB.

## Container and scene

- glTF 2.0 binary generated by Blender glTF I/O 5.1.20.
- 135 nodes.
- 39 meshes / 42 primitives.
- 27 materials.
- 21 texture entries / 20 embedded PNG images.
- One skin with 56 joints.
- Three animation clips: `idle`, `walk`, and `run`.
- No required glTF extensions.
- The authored bind pose is an A-pose, not a strict T-pose.

## Geometry and skin integrity

- Every drawable mesh node references the same `AvatarSkeleton` skin.
- Every primitive contains `JOINTS_0` and `WEIGHTS_0`.
- The skin contains 56 joints and exactly 56 inverse bind matrices.
- No invalid triangle indices were found.
- No invalid joint indices were found.
- No negative or non-finite skin weights were found.
- Per-vertex weight sums ranged from `0.9999998509883881` to `1.0000001285225153`; zero vertices were outside a `0.001` normalization tolerance.
- No non-finite position or normal values were found.
- All morph target buffers matched their base vertex counts and contained finite values.
- The body is one continuous skinned mesh; hair, clothing, shoes, accessories, and facial-detail meshes are separate and named.

## Materials and textures

- All material-to-texture, texture-to-image, and image-to-buffer-view references are valid.
- The embedded skin, brow, eyelash, hair, and shoe textures load in the viewer.
- Material coverage is incomplete by modern PBR standards: most textured materials carry base color only, two shoe materials carry normal maps, and the GLB does not provide a complete roughness/metallic/AO set for every part.
- Several shoe/hair materials use `BLEND` because their source atlases contain alpha. The application already forces closed footwear opaque at runtime; this is an authoring quirk, not a broken reference.

## Animation finding

All animation samplers and target-node references are structurally valid, but the clips are extremely sparse:

- `idle`: 4 channels, three keys each; targets only `upperarm_l`, `upperarm_r`, `spine_02`, and `spine_03`.
- `walk`: 6 channels, three keys each; targets only both upper arms, both thighs, and both calves.
- `run`: 7 channels, three keys each; targets the walk bones plus `spine_01`.

The locomotion clips do not animate the pelvis/root, feet, lower arms, hands, head, or other expected supporting joints. Viewer inspection of the unscaled original female morph shows that attachments remain skinned, but the walk is mechanically limited and the run creates severe limb/body intersection and an implausibly high leg swing. These defects are native to the original GLB and are visible without the later runtime proportion scales.

## Recommended next action

Retain the modular mesh and skin structure. Re-author or retarget locomotion from a clean A- or T-pose source onto the existing 56-bone skeleton, then validate body and every fitted mesh through a proper deformation stress set. If the external skill requires a strict T-pose, export a dedicated T-pose/rest-pose derivative while preserving node names, skin indices, morph targets, modular slot roots, materials, and the original protected GLB.
