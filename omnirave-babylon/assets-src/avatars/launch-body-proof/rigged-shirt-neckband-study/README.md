# Connected shirt collar

`male-rigged-shirt-neckband.blend` joins the two preceding pointed collar flaps with a continuous band around the neck. The connected collar is one closed, consistently wound mesh with 9,054 vertices and 18,104 triangles. Its lower rear edge flares toward the measured shirt neckline, leaving a nominal 0.5 mm vertical gap in the authored T pose. This is a fitted collar layer: it is joined to both flaps through shared vertices, but is not sewn into the original shirt mesh. The open shirt's side pattern and the band's upper transition still need tailoring refinement.

All 1,152 original flap vertices retain exact coordinates, weights and `ShirtDetailCoordinates`. Only the sixteen upper cap triangles on each flap are removed. The added band contributes 7,902 vertices; the net scene increase is 15,808 authoring triangles. All retained flap surface triangles preserve their orientation. Every other original mesh, skin, corrective shape, rig, action, transform, material and packed image remains exact, including the original shirt, embroidered jacket, seven hardware objects, placket and four buttons. No new bone, material, corrective key or driver is added.

The band is fitted between the actual T-pose neck/shirt and jacket surfaces, with a 0.35–0.65 mm radial wall. Its weights combine barycentric anchors on the original body and shirt with the preserved flap endpoint weights. Body- and shirt-derived head influence is merged into `neck_01` before retaining the strongest four influences; the original flap weights are untouched. A localized six-pass angular filter softens a sharp weight transition at the rear sides while preserving the bottom row and front joins. The independent auditor reconstructs this complete weight process and matches the saved skin exactly. Maximum discarded weight is 0.0043856%; maximum authored T-position residual is 0.2304 micrometers. Head and neck skin matrices agree within 1e-6 in every tested state; independently animated head motion remains untested.

`neckband-audit.json` checks the exact final binary across 354 established arm samples and 36 single-axis neck samples. All collar strict crossings with body, shirt, jacket, seven hardware objects, five remaining shirt details and itself are zero. No mating exceptions or relaxed contact tolerances are used. Topology has one connected component, zero boundary/nonmanifold edges, zero degenerate faces and zero winding conflicts; T signed volume is positive. The preceding shirt audit's accepted scope and model/report hashes are verified. Unchanged source geometry carries its existing garment/hardware checks forward.

These finite scopes overlap. They do not establish continuous clearance, coplanar-contact handling, arbitrary or combined motion, locomotion, independent head motion or physical cloth behavior. Maximum edge strain is 69.384% at overhead-review frame 49; this is substantial and remains a deformation-refinement limit, not a passed physical threshold. The localized neck-control strain fell from 133.919% to 40.857% after filtering. Unsigned nearest-shirt distances are descriptive: much of the band is intentionally above the shirt's open side neckline, so their maximum is not a seam-attachment error. The model remains an authoring checkpoint with general animation and runtime export unaccepted.

A fresh build using three Blender threads matches the final four-thread build exactly for all checked static geometry, raw topology/edge indices, skin, shapes, attributes, rig/action/material data and packed images. All 25 evaluated meshes match in native T and lowered poses, as do all 13 provenance arrays. The builder supplies sorted explicit edges to prevent Blender's thread-dependent edge discovery from changing internal indices; the rejected indexing comparison is retained. Nine review images show the dressed model, neck perturbation, uncovered rear fit and isolated collar geometry. Rendering preserves the saved model.

## Reproduce

Run from the repository root using Blender 5.1.2. Choose fresh output paths. The builder and auditor default to the preceding shirt-detail study; its matching `shirt-audit.json` must remain beside the source model.

```sh
blender --background --threads 3 --python-exit-code 1 --python omnirave-babylon/scripts/launch-body-proof/build_rigged_shirt_neckband.py -- --output /tmp/neckband-repeat/model.blend --provenance /tmp/neckband-repeat/provenance.npz --report /tmp/neckband-repeat/build.json
blender --background --python-exit-code 1 --python omnirave-babylon/scripts/launch-body-proof/audit_rigged_shirt_neckband.py -- --input /tmp/neckband-repeat/model.blend --provenance /tmp/neckband-repeat/provenance.npz --report /tmp/neckband-repeat/audit.json
blender --background --python-exit-code 1 --python omnirave-babylon/scripts/launch-body-proof/verify_rigged_shirt_reproduction.py -- --first omnirave-babylon/assets-src/avatars/launch-body-proof/rigged-shirt-neckband-study/male-rigged-shirt-neckband.blend --second /tmp/neckband-repeat/model.blend --first-provenance omnirave-babylon/assets-src/avatars/launch-body-proof/rigged-shirt-neckband-study/neckband-provenance.npz --second-provenance /tmp/neckband-repeat/provenance.npz --report /tmp/neckband-repeat/reproduction.json
```

`render_tailored_jacket_review.py` supplies five overall views and `render_rigged_shirt_neckband.py` supplies four collar views. Both accept `--input` and `--output` after `--`. The final model SHA is `39a81b7caabfebfca6ecea5ce05d74543a3542093662a57e806129ecc03575dc`.
